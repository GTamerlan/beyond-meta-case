# Beyond the Screen: Put the Digital World Where Work Happens

The smartphone put the world in our hands. Its successor should put that world back around us. Imagine instructions beside the product you are picking, directions beside your destination, and a workspace that follows you without a desk. Meta should pursue glasses as an everyday interface—not another notification device. The ambition is to replace the phone; the five-year strategy is to earn that role one indispensable job at a time.

## Start with the adoption problem

Early-adopter saturation is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis. Unclear purpose and cost are reported purchase barriers ([Vision Council, September 2025](https://thevisioncouncil.org/blog/latest-report-vision-council-shows-rising-awareness-smart-eyewear-recent-years)). Interview nonbuyers and trial users to separate price, discomfort, privacy, and missing utility. Google’s everyday AI glasses and XREAL’s laptop-connected spatial displays challenge different parts of Meta’s opportunity ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/android/android-xr-io-2026/); [XREAL](https://www.xreal.com/uk/blog/project-aura-google-io-2026), May 2026). Winning requires complete workflows, not simply smaller hardware.

## Prioritize workers with a reason to switch

First, target frequent independent grocery-shopping and delivery workers at participating retailers. Their work repeatedly switches between physical tasks and phone instructions. Instacart reported approximately **600,000 active shoppers in December 2025**, defined as completing at least one order that month ([2025 Form 10-K, February 26, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1579091/000157909126000018/cart-20251231.htm)). This is a reachable distribution channel, not a forecast of buyers.

Adjacent labor demand strengthens the case for testing work applications: BLS projects **8.9%** employment growth for stockers and order fillers and **6.3%** for light truck drivers, versus **3.5%** overall, during 2025–2035 ([BLS, August 27, 2026](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm)). These broader occupations are not the addressable consumer market.

Second, target freelancers and consultants moving between client sites and shared offices. Offer portable screen space only when readability and sustained comfort justify replacing their existing setup.

## 1. Build a lighter, more natural interface

Combine transparent displays, precise eye tracking, and distributed computing. Orion demonstrates Meta’s direction, not consumer readiness ([Meta, September 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/09/introducing-orion-our-first-true-augmented-reality-glasses/)). Keep fast tracking local; offload heavier processing to a phone or pocket module, as Qualcomm’s architecture supports ([Qualcomm, November 2022](https://www.qualcomm.com/news/releases/2022/11/qualcomm-launches-snapdragon-ar2-designed-to-revolutionize-ar-gl)).

Invest specifically in nanophotonics: patterned surfaces that control light. A research prototype used couplers etched from a **160-nanometer film on a 1-millimeter full-color waveguide** ([Tian et al., February 25, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41377-025-01761-w)). That film is not a complete glasses display. Stanford’s metasurface-waveguide holography removed bulky collimation optics, but viewing range and computation remained constraints ([Nature, May 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07386-0)). Compare alternative optical architectures on brightness, text clarity, manufacturing yield, heat, battery, and cost; do not make adoption depend on a speculative breakthrough.

Let gaze point and deliberate input confirm. Wrist EMG has peer-reviewed support ([Nature, July 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09255-w)). Add an optional tactile Bluetooth ring for clicking and thumb scrolling, plus voice and physical fallbacks. Treat facial-muscle commands as exploratory, not a launch dependency.

## 2. Sell a better shift, not another gadget

Propose an Instacart integration for item guidance, barcode confirmation, substitutions, and translated messages. Prioritize retailers supplying reliable aisle and shelf data; those fields already exist but are optional ([Instacart, August 2026](https://docs.instacart.com/catalog/catalog_inventory_file/specifications/in-store-locations)). Show one instruction at a time. Disable interactive overlays while driving; Amazon’s developing delivery glasses activate after parking ([Amazon, October 2025](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/transportation/smart-glasses-amazon-delivery-drivers)).

For professionals, connect approved computer applications to spatial displays and retain keyboards for sustained writing. An opt-in assistant can learn explicitly shared routines, calendars, and preferences. Give users editable memory and confirmation before purchases, messages, or bookings. Keep worker participation voluntary; exclude gaze monetization and employer access to raw behavioral data.

DHL reported **25% higher picking efficiency** in a three-week, ten-worker warehouse pilot ([DHL, January 2015](https://www.dhl.com/global-en/delivered/innovation/dhl-successfully-tests-augmented-reality-application-in-warehouse.html)). That historical result motivates testing—not a promised gain for shoppers.

## 3. Make the benefit easy to try

Preserve affordable audio glasses; introduce a premium AR tier when utility earns its price. Use platform-funded loaners, prescription fitting, clear returns, and transparent financing. Keep basic controls subscription-free. Offer shopper trials through platforms and retailers, then professional demonstrations through coworking partners. Base subsidies on measured partner value, not compulsory worker debt.

## Invest in stages

Year one: compare phone and glasses shifts on supported hardware, measuring task time, errors, comfort, safety, phone interactions, and paid conversion. Years two–three: scale only with sustained voluntary use and positive margins after support, returns, and AI costs; test professional workspaces against existing alternatives. Years four–five: expand spatial applications as battery, affordability, reliability, and privacy permit.

The next interface should expand what people can do—not how much attention a device can take.
